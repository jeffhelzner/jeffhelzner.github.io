What I build

Bayesian and ML decision models

Models that combine benchmark data with sparse, account-level evidence to produce defensible estimates with honest uncertainty. For example: Bayesian loss picks that blend class-rate priors with submission-level loss and payroll data.

AI tools that augment experts

LLM-powered applications built around how adjusters and underwriters actually work: drafting claim status updates, standardizing and imputing fields in OCR-extracted loss records, and presenting model output in forms experts trust and use.

Data pipelines and monitoring

The infrastructure decisions depend on: ingestion and transformation of underwriting and exposure data, automated checks that flag anomalous shifts in claim frequency or severity, and dashboards tied to the decisions they inform.

Evaluation of decision quality

Methods for checking whether a decision maker, human or AI, is consistent, coherent, and responsive to evidence: noise audits of expert judgment, and Bayesian measures of how closely LLM choices track expected utility.

Research and public work

I've been working on one question since my graduate training in logic and decision theory at Carnegie Mellon: what does it mean to decide well under uncertainty, and how can you tell? At Columbia I studied it formally. At AIG, working with Daniel Kahneman, I measured it in experienced underwriters and adjusters. Now that AI systems are making real decisions, I apply the same discipline to them, turning "this system decides well" into something you can estimate, check, and improve.

Working paper and open-source project · 2026

Sensitivity to Subjective Expected Utility Maximization: A Methodological Study, with an Illustrative Application to LLM Decision-Making

A Bayesian framework for estimating how strongly a decision maker's choices track expected utility, together with the adequacy checks that determine when that estimate can be trusted. Illustrated on LLM decision-making under varying sampling temperature.
